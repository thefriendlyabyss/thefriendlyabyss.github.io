## A Month of Island History

We spent a week in September on the history of the Isle of Pines, the island's name for most of the twentieth century. We looked at the grapefruit that made the island famous back home, including a 1914 USDA watercolor of a variety actually called the "Isle of Pines," and at the island's pirate past, when Henry Morgan used it in 1668 to launch his attack on Puerto Príncipe.

We also picked up a new source: Morton D. Winsberg's 1958 University of Florida dissertation, *Isle of Pines, Cuba: A Geographic Interpretation*. It fills in a lot of what came before and around the American colony, and it sits right alongside Michael Neagle's *America's Forgotten Colony* as one of the foundations we're building on.

Then we gave a week to the films that shaped this one: *Aftersun*, *Y Tu Mamá También*, and *Paris, Texas*. Three very different movies, all about journeys, memory, and what people can't quite say to each other.

## Getting Ready for December

A lot of this month happened where nobody can see it. Crew agreements, production insurance that actually covers filming in Cuba, travel planning, and making sure every step of this production stays fully within U.S. regulations for filming on the island. None of it makes for a great Instagram post, but it's what lets us get there, shoot, and come back with a film.

We also kept working on grant applications, with a couple submitted and more in progress.
